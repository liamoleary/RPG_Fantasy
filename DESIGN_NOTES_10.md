# BANNERFELL — Design Notes 10: The Long March (the climb carries your warband)

Companion to `GAME_DESIGN.md` and `DESIGN_NOTES_01–09`. **This amends DN09**: the tier ladder, its authored modifiers, the crest, the records and the telemetry all survive exactly as merged — what changes is *when a tier is entered*. Where DN09 §2 and §4 conflict with this document, this document wins.

---

## 1. The playtest overruled the designer, and the designer agrees

DN09 §2 rejected carrying the warband between tiers, and play immediately exposed both halves of that ruling:

1. **Fresh-starting into Tier 2 is miserable.** The harness said Veteran Rivals was +0.2 — but the harness player can't feel a zero-noise AI the way a human does. Restarting from three militia against visibly sharper rivals, immediately after commanding a full late-game warband, is a double punishment: you lost your army *and* the game got harder. The reward for winning was to be made small again, at altitude.
2. **DN09's core objection no longer holds, because DN08 changed the math.** The "nothing left to build" argument was made against the old game, where a winner's board was finished. Post-DN08, the measured *winner* finishes at 5.3 of 9 possible slots, 3.1 of 5 talent tiers, ~0.2 Honored stacks and zero Legendaries, with Standards uncapped. A victorious warband has enormous headroom — the draft arc does not die at the first victory anymore. I was defending a constraint the game no longer has.

What survives from DN09's reasoning: sessions must stay stoppable (§4 fixes this with resume, not shorter ambitions), and difficulty must be authored against a known power level (§5 replaces "whatever you finished with" tuning with explicit arrival budgets).

## 2. The Long March — one campaign, one warband, rising tiers

A run becomes a **campaign**. It begins at Tier 1 exactly as today. On winning a lobby at Tier N, the victory screen offers:

- **March On — Tier N+1** *(headline)*: your warband, hero, talents, ranks, Standards and remaining slots all continue into a **new lobby of eight fresh rivals** under Tier N+1's cumulative banner rules (unchanged from the merged `tiers.ts`).
- **Claim Victory**: end the campaign now, banked at Tier N.

The campaign ends when you fall or claim. The defeat screen already leads with altitude ("You fell at War Tier 3") — now it's literally true of the warband you carried there. The Home crest shows the highest tier **reached by any campaign** (DN09's records re-keyed from "runs at tier" to "campaigns reaching tier"). Renown multiplier per DN09 §4, applied per tier climbed, win or lose.

Campaigns always start at Tier 1. That's deliberate: Tier 1 is the beloved draft arc, ~20 minutes, and it's where each campaign's identity (faction, lines, tree shape) gets forged. A veteran shortcut ("begin at Tier N−2") is explicitly deferred until telemetry shows T1 replay fatigue actually exists.

## 3. The Interlude — between lobbies, the army rests

Entering a new tier plays a short **Interlude** (one screen, not a phase):

- **Banner HP restores to full.** Each lobby is a fresh war; carrying HP debt across tiers would make early-tier sloppiness compound into unwinnable altitude — the tier rules are the difficulty, not attrition bookkeeping.
- **Gold resets to the tier's opening stipend** (base income + one War Chest–sized grant, exact number sim-tuned). No gold banking across tiers — hoarding at an easy tier to buy out a hard one would flatten the climb.
- **+1 bonus talent point** on arrival at each new tier (on top of the level-up cadence, which continues on round count within the new lobby). A campaign that reaches Tier 3–4 finishes its tree — the War Council's full ladder finally gets walked, which DN08 measured almost never happens in one lobby.
- The warband itself is untouched: stacks, counts, ranks, promotions, Standards, opened slots all persist. **Nothing the player built is ever taken away by the climb.**

## 4. Sessions — a campaign is many sittings

A campaign at Tier 4 might be 80+ minutes of total play. That's fine **because it was never one sitting**: `activeRun` resume already survives app close mid-lobby, and the Interlude is a natural stopping point ("March On" can be answered tomorrow — the choice persists). The UI should embrace it: Home shows an in-progress campaign as a banner card ("Tier 3 — round 4 — resume the march") above New Campaign. One campaign in flight at a time, per today's single-activeRun model.

## 5. Rivals at altitude — arrival budgets, not mirrors

New-lobby rivals at Tier N start with **pre-developed warbands built to an authored arrival budget** — a per-tier power curve (units, tiers, counts, talent points) calibrated so a *typical* tier-winning warband arrives as a strong contender, not a bully or a victim. Rules:

- Budgets are authored data (`data/arrival.ts`), per tier, validated by the harness — never generated by copying or scaling the player's actual board (mirror-scaling makes building pointless: DN09 §2.3's "tuning cliff" objection, solved by fixing the reference point instead of abandoning carry).
- Rivals then develop normally through the lobby (policies, tier rules, their own arrival talents).
- The lobby's round counter restarts at 1 for economy/level-up cadence, but **banner damage's round term continues from the campaign's total round count** — late-campaign losses stay lethal, so a Tier 3 lobby can't be coasted on a fat HP pool.

## 6. What this asks of balance

- The harness campaigns now: policy plays lobby → marches → repeat until it falls. New columns: tiers reached distribution, per-tier win rate *of arriving warbands*, arrival-budget delta (how far above/below budget real arriving boards sit). Targets: a competent policy falls at median Tier 2–3; arrival delta within ±15% of budget at tiers 2–4.
- Tier 2's felt difficulty resolves structurally (you face Veteran Rivals with your winning army), but re-check the whole DN09 §6 curve shape under carry — the ladder's bite must now come from budgets + banner rules together.
- The DN09 §7.2 discipline stands: per-tier faction spread stays in band; Growth compounding across a long campaign is the number-one thing to watch (a Verdant warband that marches three tiers has had three lobbies of Growth ticks — if Verdant's tiers-reached distribution pulls away, cap Growth ticks per campaign, not per lobby).

## 7. Acceptance criteria

1. Winning any lobby offers March On / Claim; marching preserves the entire warband state (unit-for-unit fingerprint test) and applies exactly the next tier's cumulative rules.
2. The Interlude applies full HP, stipend gold, +1 talent point; nothing else changes without being listed on the Interlude screen.
3. A campaign can be closed at the Interlude and resumed days later from Home; mid-lobby resume unchanged.
4. Records, crest, defeat screens and telemetry are re-keyed to campaigns; DN09's tier picker becomes the campaign-history view (per-tier reached/fallen counts).
5. Arrival budgets are data; the harness campaign report shows tiers-reached distribution, per-tier arrival win rates, and per-tier faction spread in band.
6. Tier 1 remains fingerprint-identical for a campaign that never marches; all existing tests pass; determinism per seed holds across the March On boundary (the new lobby's seed derives from the campaign seed + tier).
7. No content gated behind tiers; multiplier applies win or lose (both inherited from DN09, re-tested under carry).
